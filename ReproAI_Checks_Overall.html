ReproAI Checks: An Overview of AI-led Reproducibility Audits

Summary across three sources of studies — I4R, SCORE, and additional sources

Across 17 audited studies, 0 critical (P0) findings; reproducibility varies strongly with whether the authors shared data and code.

Abstract

This report summarizes AI-led reproducibility audits of 17 studies for which a full-text PDF was available and an audit was completed: 5 I4R, 10 SCORE, and 2 additional studies. Audits were performed by DeepSeek V4 Flash via uniGPT within the ReproAI pipeline (engine: anomalyco/opencode), following a severity classification of P0 (critical) to P3 (minor).

No study received a P0 (critical) finding. Headline results were independently reproduced for 4 studies and reproduced or found internally consistent (with only minor-to-moderate issues) for a further 5. 2 studies were only partially reproducible, and 6 could not be independently reproduced because no data or code was shared or obtainable. Across the whole sample, the single most common P1 (major) issue was the absence of a public data/code deposit, not a demonstrated error in the reported statistics.

Introduction

This document gives a summary and overview of the outcomes of ReproAI checks run on three batches of studies. It is deliberately kept as an overview rather than a full manuscript.

The overarching question is descriptive and practical: for how many studies could the claimed results be independently checked, and of those, for how many did the check work versus reveal issues or big problems? The answer depends almost entirely on whether a full text was obtainable and whether the authors shared data and code.

Method

Target-of-analysis selection

Only studies for which a full-text PDF was available and a complete audit was performed are included. Studies whose full text remained paywalled and was never provided to the auditor are excluded from this overview (they are listed in the per-batch reports).

ReproAI audit procedure

For each included study, audits (authored and executed by DeepSeek V4 Flash via uniGPT) comprised: (a) a full-text claim inventory; (b) independent re-implementation of headline analyses in R or Python where the data/code were obtainable; (c) internal-consistency arithmetic checks (tables vs text, sample sizes, direction claims, percentages); and (d) a severity-classified findings list (P0 critical, P1 major, P2 moderate, P3 minor). A claim is marked “independently reproducible” only if the headline result was re-derived from the authors’ shared materials.

Transparency of authorship

All checks in this report were performed by DeepSeek V4 Flash via uniGPT, following the ReproAI discipline and rules version 2026.06.27.

Discussion

Across the 17 audited studies there were no P0 (critical) findings — that is, no independently demonstrated error that overturns a headline conclusion. Where reproduction was attempted from shared data, results generally confirmed the reported statistics; the clearest fully-reproducible cases (e.g., the Nature Human Behaviour umbrella review, two APSR/JOP empirical papers, and the Sociological Science field-vs-vignette study) shipped code and data, sometimes with a preregistration.

The dominant P1 finding, however, is not a numerical error but an availability gap: 6 studies could not be independently reproduced because no data or code was deposited or obtainable (several preprints promised but never shipped them). A smaller number showed internal inconsistencies or presentation errors (mislabeled table columns, duplicated rows, prose-figure mismatches) that did not change the substantive conclusion.

This overview therefore illustrates a familiar pattern for AI-led reproducibility checking: the detectable outcomes are strongly gated by data/code availability, while the largely assumed non-reproducibility of the field is concentrated in exactly the studies that provide no shared materials to check.
