Meta-Psychology Reprochecks: Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria & Flowchart

ReproAI study · Applied to Meta-Psychology articles (Vols 3–7 onward, published at open.lnu.se)

1. Study aim

For every article published in Meta-Psychology, we (a) retrieve the paper and its metadata, (b) determine whether it contains independently verifiable numerical claims, and (c) if so, re-implement the reported analyses to check whether the results reproduce. Non-empirical pieces are flagged and recorded but not re-run.

2. Inclusion criteria

#CriterionOperational rule
INC-1Journal of recordArticle is published in Meta-Psychology (open.lnu.se), i.e., has a DOI 10.15626/MP.* and a journal header on page 1.
INC-2Volume/periodArticle falls within the study window (starting Vol 3, 2019; proceeding forward by volume).
INC-3Full text obtainableA PDF galley is downloadable from the journal; full text can be extracted.
INC-4Verifiable numerical claimsThe article reports statistics/estimates derived from data, simulation, or a fully-specified worked example that can be recomputed.

3. Exclusion criteria

#CriterionOperational rule
EXC-1Non-empirical article typeArticle type is Commentary, Editorial, Guest Editorial, Tutorial, Review Protocol, Framework/guidance, Tool/method description, or Conceptual/theoretical → no reprocheck.
EXC-2No verifiable numbersFull-text audit finds no recomputable numeric claim (purely argumentative or descriptive prose).
EXC-3Data & method unavailableThe data/code is not available AND the method cannot be independently reconstructed to a defensible degree.
EXC-4Descriptive/investigative onlyAn article with only descriptive statistics and no reproducible analysis pipeline (e.g., investigative case studies) is treated as non-empirical.

4. Empirical vs non-empirical classification

An article is empirical (goes on to reprocheck) if it reports results that a third party could recompute, i.e. it is a: replication report, original article with data, meta-analysis, file-drawer report, or a methods/simulation article with reproducible output. An article is non-empirical (recorded, not re-run) if it is commentary, editorial, tutorial, conceptual, framework/guidance, tool description, review protocol, or descriptive without a reproducible analysis.
