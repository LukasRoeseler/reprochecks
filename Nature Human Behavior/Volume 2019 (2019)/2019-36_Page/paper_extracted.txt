Testing adaptive hypotheses of alloparenting
in Agta foragers
Abigail E. Page 1,2*, Matthew G. Thomas 2, Daniel Smith 3, Mark Dyble 4, Sylvain Viguier2,
Nikhil Chaudhary 5, Gul Deniz Salali 2, James Thompson2, Ruth Mace 2 and Andrea B. Migliano 6
Human children are frequently cared for by non-parental caregivers (alloparents), yet few studies have conducted system-
atic alternative hypothesis tests of why alloparents help. Here we explore whether predictions from kin selection, reciprocity,
learning-to-mother and costly signalling hypotheses explain non-parental childcare among Agta hunter-gatherers from the
Philippines. To test these hypotheses, we used high-resolution proximity data from 1,701 child–alloparent dyads. Our results
indicated that reciprocity and relatedness were positively associated with the number of interactions with a child (our proxy
for childcare). Need appeared more influential in close kin, suggesting indirect benefits, while reciprocity proved to be a stron-
ger influence in non-kin, pointing to direct benefits. However, despite shared genes, close and distant kin interactions were
also contingent on reciprocity. Compared with other apes, humans are unique in rapidly producing energetically demanding
offspring. Our results suggest that the support that mothers require is met through support based on kinship and reciprocity.

Study description Study type is quantitative
Research sample The research sample was based from a population of 914 Agta foragers from North-eastern Philippines. In such a small population the
sample was dependent on which villages/camps were had the time to visit (who we met) and who agreed to take part in the study. The
study consisted of 85 children (aged 0.08-11 years, 41.9% female) and 147 childcarers (aged 6.22-75 years, 50.9% female)
Sampling strategy As this research is on a population of very limited population the sample size is dependent mainly on the time and fieldwork constraints
(time take to travel to remote camps) and whether individuals agreed to take part in the study. As a result, no formal statistical methods
were used to estimate sample size. However, the paper does not lack from an issue of sample size as while the data is collected from 85
children and 147 childcarers, the point of analysis is on the dyadic relationship between these individuals. As a result, the total sample
size was 1,701 for one set of models and 1615 for another. Therefore, the analysis is not under-powered.
Data collection These behavioral observational occurred in a natural setting (the participants every day lives continued as normal) therefore there were
many individuals around at the same time as the data collection occurred (as is the point of the data collection). Interaction data was
collected using the UCMote Mini (with a TinyOS operating system). The motes were sealed into wristbands and belts (depending on size
and preference) and labeled with a unique number and identified with colored string to avoid accidental swaps. Additional data
collection was occurred as followed: focal follows of children = pen and paper and genealogies = pen and paper,
Timing Motes data collection occurred continuously in different villages/camps from February to October 2014. Within the camps data
collection occurred over a seven day period with the motes recording interactions between 05:00 and 20:00 on each of these days.
Data exclusions The motes stored messages within a three meter radius. However for increased robustness, we used a subset of the interactions which
occured within 1.5 meters to ensure 'interactions' recorded where meaningful events. Therefore, any interactions more distant than 1.5
meters were excluded from the analysis. No other data was excluded.
Non-participation No records were kept of individuals who did not want to participate. Many reasons was being uncomfortable wearing the motes as an
unfamiliar device.

Population characteristics There are around a total of 1,000 Agta living in Palanan municipality in north-eastern Luzon, we met and worked with 914 of
them. Riverine and marine spearfishing provides the primary source of animal protein, supplemented by inter-tidal foraging and
the gathering of wild foods as well as low-intensity cultivation, wage labour and trade. Further demographic details of the
sample can be found above.
Recruitment We went to different camps where individuals lived and would introduce ourselves and arrange a community meeting in which
we would show posters and act out/explain what it was we were doing and why. During this time we would collect informed
consents from each of the adults who would sign on behalf of their children.
Ethics oversight This research was approved by UCL Ethics Committee (UCL Ethics code 3086/003) and carried out with permission from local
government and tribal leaders. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, after group and individual consultation and
