Fusion with political leaders predicts willingness
to persecute immigrants and political opponents
Jonas R. Kunst 1,2,3*, John F. Dovidio2 and Lotte Thomsen1,4
From the 2016 US presidential election and into 2019, we demonstrate that a visceral feeling of oneness (that is, psychologi-
cal fusion) with a political leader can fuel partisans’ willingness to actively participate in political violence. In studies 1 and 2,
fusion with Donald Trump predicted Republicans’ willingness to violently persecute Muslims (over and above other established
predictors). In study 3, relative deprivation increased fusion with Trump and, subsequently, willingness to violently challenge
election results. In study 4, fusion with Trump increased after his election and predicted immigrant persecution over time.
Further revealing its independent effects, this fusion with Trump predicted a willingness to persecute Iranians (independent of
identification with him, study 5); a willingness to persecute immigrants (study 6); and a willingness to personally protect the
US border from an immigrant caravan (study 7), even over and above fusion with the group of Trump’s followers. These findings
echo past political movements and suggest critical future research.

Study description Quantitative. Correlational, experimental and longitudinal.
Research sample In all studies reported, participants are self-identified Republicans recruited through Amazon MTurk, except for Study 2 for which the
sample was recruited through Qualtrics Panels. This sampling strategy was chosen as it has shown to secure high-quality data. Given that
participants come from a standing pool of participants, these samples are not representative, except for Study 2 that was based on a
nationally-representative sample
Study 1: 205 White American participants (Mage = 42.46, SDage = 13.16; women = 57.1%) reporting a Republican political affiliation
Study 2: 385 White Americans (Mage = 49.34, SDage = 14.79; women = 50.1%) with a Republican political affiliation
Study 3: 301 White American participants indicating a Republican political affiliation (Mage = 42.74, SDage = 12.46; 59.5% women).
Study 4: 194 Republicans (Mage = 40.44, SDage = 12.44; 61.3% women; 83.5% White/Caucasian)
Study 5: 176 Republicans (Mage = 43.19, SDage = 12.76; 58.5% women; 89.8% White/Caucasian)
Study 6: 171 Republicans (Mage = 43.18, SDage = 12.64; 50.2% women; 84.8% White/Caucasian)
Study 7: Using Amazon MTurk’s built-in political affiliation selection criteria to recruit 176 Republicans (Mage = 45.09, SDage = 13.05;
53.4% women; 84.1% White/Caucasian)
Sampling strategy For all studies except for Study 2: Participants see the studies on the MTurk platform and can opt-in to participate.
For Study 2: Qualtrics Panels recruits participants through their business partners.
All sample sizes were based on power analyses/criteria.
Study 1: A power analysis in G*Power 3.1.9.2 indicated that 171 participants would warrant a 90% chance to observe a small to medium
effect (f2 = .10; α = .05) in regression-based analyses.
Study 2: Same as Study 1.
Study 3: Because we used a novel experimental manipulation, we aimed to recruit more than 266 participants, which would provide a
90% chance to observe a small to medium effect (f = .20; α = .05) in analyses of variance (ANOVAs).
Study 4: As part of this study involved the estimation of an autoregressive model with nine manifest variables, we aimed to recruit 100 to
150 participants who completed each wave, following suggestions by Wang and Wang and satisfying a 10/1 ratio between the number of
participants and observed variables. To account for a possible attrition rate of up to 30% from T1 to T3, we planned to recruit
approximately 200 participants at T1.
Study 5-7: Same power analytic rationale as Study 1.
Data collection Data was collected through an online survey hosted by Qualtrics.
Timing Study 1: September 2016
Study 2: April 2019
Study 3: October 2016
Study 4: Period between one week before and one week after Election Day 2016 (November 8)
Study 5: February 2019
Study 6: October 2018
Study 7: October 2018
Data exclusions No data was excluded from analyses.
Non-participation We do not have information about this. Normally these rates are very low for studies on Amazon Mturk, as people opt-in to take part in
