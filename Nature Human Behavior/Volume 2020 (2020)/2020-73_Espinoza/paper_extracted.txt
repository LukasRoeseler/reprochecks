Establishing the structure and replicability of
personality profiles using the HEXACO-PI-R
Jose A. Espinoza 1,3 ✉, Kabir N. Daljeet 1,3 ✉ and John P. Meyer 1,2
Previous attempts to identify personality profiles in the five-factor and HEXACO models of personality have produced incon-
sistent results. Here, using data from four independent samples, each with approximately 90,000 international respondents
to the 100-item HEXACO Personality Inventory-Revised (HEXACO-PI-R), we demonstrated that a five-profile solution fit the
data well. Exploratory analyses suggested that this solution was also consistent across gender and age groups. The five-profile
structure replicated well with larger subsamples, but could not be reproduced consistently with samples of fewer than 500 indi-
viduals. However, even with small samples, the five-profile structure could be applied using the parameters obtained with the
larger samples. We used HEXACO theory along with agency–communion and attachment theories to offer preliminary expla-
nations and labels for the five profiles. We discuss how these theories, combined with parameter estimates provided by our
research, can be used to generate and test hypotheses to validate the five-profile structure and evaluate its utility for personal-
ity research and other applications.

indicators for the three- to ten-profile solutions in the initial sample
(n = 90,289). To identify the correct number of profiles to extract, we
examined four important statistical indices of fit: AIC59, CAIC60, BIC61 and
SABIC62. The lower the value of these fit statistics, the better the model fit.
These statistical indicators are plotted and visually inspected for a point of
inflection, which appears as an ‘elbow’ in the fit statistics on the graph. This
drastic flattening of the fit statistics suggests an optimal solution. However,
as our initial sample was very large, the best-fitting model could not be
identified on the basis of these fit statistics, as they continued to decline as
the number of profiles increased.

According to Ashton and Lee39, the HEXACO traits can be
divided into two distinct clusters. The first cluster includes hon-
esty–humility, agreeableness and emotionality, all of which have
an altruistic social focus. The second cluster includes extraversion,
conscientiousness and openness to experience which, in contrast
to the first cluster, have more of a task focus. Ashton and Lee
argued that these trait clusters have parallels in various theories of
personality and human functioning and may have unique biologi-
cal bases. For example, the altruistic social cluster includes traits
reflecting a tendency toward reciprocal caring that was essential
for nurturing and protection of the young and is most prevalent
in females. By contrast, traits in the task-focused cluster foster
engagement and accomplishment in social- (extraversion), task-
(conscientiousness) and idea-related (openness to experience)
endeavours. Therefore, it is interesting to note that three of the
five profiles obtained in our study reflect above-average scores on
traits in one cluster and below-average scores on traits in the other
cluster. That is, profile 1 includes above-average scores on con-
scientiousness and well-below-average scores on honesty–humil-
ity, agreeableness and emotionality. Profile 2 is characterized by
an above-average score on extraversion and below-average scores
on honesty–humility, emotionality and agreeableness. By con-

Study description This research uses a large dataset of individual responses to the HEXACO-100 measure of personality to identify subgroups that reflect
combinations (profiles) of the HEXACO traits present in the sample. We use various techniques using subsamples and holdout samples of
the dataset to examine the structure and replicability of multiple profile solutions and identify the optimal solution to the data. We also
conduct exploratory analyses to determine the estimated proportions of individuals in each profile in the optimal profile solution
according to gender and age.
Research sample The dataset used in this sample was collected through an online portal by the creators of the HEXACO model of personality (Lee &
Ashton, 2018; www.hexaco.org). The initial dataset is large (n = 100,318) and contains responses from individuals with a wide range of
ages, ethnicities, and gender. As such, we expected that it provided a reasonable estimation of the population of HEXACO test takers
allowing us to address concerns with previous research in this area conducted with small (N < 500) and non-representative samples (e.g.,
only university students). An additional independent dataset (n = 270,539) was used to examine the replicability of the solution identified
in the initial sample. Participants responded to the complete measure of the HEXACO model, the HEXACO-100, assessing individuals on
the six HEXACO personality traits (Honesty-Humility, Emotionality, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, and Openness to
Experience). All responses were made on a 5-point Likert-type scale from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree) and each HEXACO
trait was assessed using 16 items, with an additional 4 items used to assess the interstitial facet of Altruism.
Sampling strategy We use a dataset collected in a previous study. Sampling is described in the original study.
Data collection Data was collected in a previous study and its collection is described in Lee and Ashton (2018):
Lee, K., & Ashton, M. C. (2018). Psychometric Properties of the HEXACO-100. Assessment,
25(5), 1-15. https://doi.org/10.1177/1073191116659134
Timing We use a dataset collected in a previous study. Sampling is described in the original study.
Data exclusions We used only the data from the 100,318 (of 104,467) who completed all 100 items from the HEXACO-100 and correctly responded to
three attentiveness-check items. Details regarding data collection and data cleansing are reported in Lee and Ashton (2018). The
additional independent dataset (n = 270,539) was provided to us after data cleansing.
