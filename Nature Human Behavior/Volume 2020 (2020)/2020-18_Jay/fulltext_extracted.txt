Neighbourhood income and physical distancing
during the COVID-19 pandemic in the
United States
Jonathan Jay 1 ✉, Jacob Bor2,3, Elaine O. Nsoesie 2, Sarah K. Lipson 4, David K. Jones 4,
Sandro Galea 3,5 and Julia Raifman4
Physical distancing has been the primary strategy to control COVID-19 in the United States. We used mobility data from a
large, anonymized sample of smartphone users to assess the relationship between neighbourhood income and physical dis-
tancing during the pandemic. We found a strong gradient between neighbourhood income and physical distancing. Individuals
in high-income neighbourhoods increased their days at home substantially more than individuals in low-income neighbour-
hoods did. Residents of low-income neighbourhoods were more likely to work outside the home, compared to residents in
higher-income neighbourhoods, but were not more likely to visit locations such as supermarkets, parks and hospitals. Finally,
we found that state orders were only associated with small increases in staying home in low-income neighbourhoods. Our find-
ings indicate that people in lower-income neighbourhoods have faced barriers to physical distancing, particularly needing to
work outside the home, and that state physical distancing policies have not mitigated these disparities.

Study description We quantitatively analyzed changes in mobility associated with neighborhood income during the COVID-19 pandemic in the U.S.
Research sample We used smartphone mobility data collected by the company SafeGraph and shared for research purposes. We used 2 SafeGraph
datasets: one containing mobility data at the census block group level for 99.6% of census block groups in the U.S. (n = 210,288), and
another containing visits to individual points of interest (e.g. parks, supermarkets, hospitals) (n = 414,946).
Sampling strategy These data are based on a convenience sample. SafeGraph aggregates anonymized location data from smartphone applications.
Data collection The researchers did not participate in data collection. SafeGraph obtains smartphone "pings" from devices whose users have enabled
location services when using certain apps. The large size of the SafeGraph sample allows highly powered analyses.
Timing Most analyses use data from January 6, 2020 through May 3, 2020, except as otherwise specified.
Data exclusions Data from February 25, 2020 was omitted, due to documented problems in the SafeGraph data from this date.
Non-participation Non-participation rates are unknown.
Randomization No randomization occurred.
