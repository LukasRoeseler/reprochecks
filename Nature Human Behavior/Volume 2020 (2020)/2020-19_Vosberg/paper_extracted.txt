Sex continuum in the brain and body during
adolescence and psychological traits
Daniel E. Vosberg 1,2, Catriona Syme2, Nadine Parker 1,3, Louis Richer 4, Zdenka Pausova 2,5 and
Tomáš Paus 1,3,6 ✉
Many traits of the brain and body show marked sex differences, but the distributions of their values overlap substantially
between the two sexes. To investigate variations associated with biological sex, beyond binary differences, we create continu-
ous sex scores capturing the inter-individual variability in phenotypes. In an adolescent cohort (n = 1,029; 533 females), we
have generated three sex scores based on brain–body traits: ‘overall’ (48 traits), ‘pubertal’ (26 traits) and ‘non-pubertal’ (22
traits). We then conducted sex-stratified multiple linear regressions (adjusting for age) using sex scores to test associations
with sex hormones, personality traits and internalizing–externalizing behaviour. Higher sex scores (that is, greater ‘female-
ness’) were associated with lower testosterone in males only, as well as lower extraversion, higher internalizing and lower
externalizing in both sexes. The associations with testosterone, internalizing and externalizing were driven by pubertal sex
scores, underscoring the importance of adolescence in shaping within-sex individual variability.

Study description Quantitative data acquired from traits in a cross-sectional adolescent cohort related to the following domains: anthropometry, brain,
cardiovascular system, face, food intake, metabolic traits, voice, sex hormones, personality, and psychopathology.
Research sample Saguenay Youth Study adolescent cohort (n = 1029; age range: 12-19 years; 533 females), recruited from the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-
Jean region of Quebec, Canada (Pausova et al., 2017, International Journal of Epidemiology).
Sampling strategy A community-based sampling procedure was conducted, by evaluating interest and eligibility from adolescents at high schools across
the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean region. See "non-participation" section for description of recruitment and sample size.
Data collection Across multiple sessions of approximately 15 hours, the following data were collected: blood samples, brain and abdominal magnetic
resonance imaging (MRI), cardiovascular evaluation, and cognitive function and personality assessment. The tools included the MRI
scanner, psychopathology assessments (DPS), a personality test (NEO-PI-R), a finometer (cardiovascular assessment), blood samples
(hormones, metabolism), and food recall.
Timing Participants were assessed between the years 2003-2012.
Data exclusions MRI data which failed visually-inspected quality control procedures were excluded. Metabolic values below the detection limit were
excluded. Values across phenotypes considered extreme outliers (greater than or equal to 4 standard deviations from the mean)
were excluded.
Non-participation Of the 18127 families (27190 students) initially contacted, 5570 responded, 3269 indicated interest, 1801 were eligible, and 481
families (1029 adolescents) participated in the study.
Randomization This manuscript did not draw comparisons between an experimental and control group; instead we conducted sex-stratified analyses
within each sex.

Preprocessing
Preprocessing software FreeSurfer was used to compute cortical surface area and thickness. Preprocessing included skull stripping, B1 bias field
correction, as well as segmentation of grey matter and white matter (https://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/fswiki/
FreeSurferWiki).
An in-house pipeline was used to compute grey matter and white matter volumes (Paus et al., 2010). Brain extraction was
performed with BET from FSL (http://fsl.oxford.ac.uk), and voxels were automatically segmented as grey matter, white
matter, or cerebrospinal fluid.
Normalization FreeSurfer: non-linear transformation
In-house pipeline: non-linear transformation
Normalization template FreeSurfer normalization: MNI305 standardized space.
For the in-house grey matter/white matter volume pipeline, an average brain computed from our population was used.
Noise and artifact removal Quality control was conducted with visual inspection of the MRI data.
