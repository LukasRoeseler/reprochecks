Chemical evidence of dairying by hunter-gatherers
in highland Lesotho in the late first millennium
ad
Helen Fewlass 1,5 ✉, Peter J. Mitchell2,3, Emmanuelle Casanova 4 and Lucy J. E. Cramp 1
The recovery of Early Iron Age artefacts and domestic animal remains from hunter-gatherer contexts at Likoaeng, Lesotho, has
been argued to indicate contact between highland hunter-gatherers and Early Iron Age agropastoralist communities settled
in lowland areas of southeastern Africa during the second half of the first millennium ad. However, disagreement between
archaeozoological studies and ancient DNA means that the possibility that those hunter-gatherers kept livestock themselves
remains controversial. Here we report analyses of pottery-absorbed organic residues from two hunter-gatherer sites and one
agriculturalist site in highland Lesotho to reconstruct prehistoric subsistence practices. Our results demonstrate the exploita-
tion of secondary products from domestic livestock by hunter-gatherers in Lesotho, directly dated to the seventh century ad at
Likoaeng and the tenth century ad at the nearby site of Sehonghong. The data provide compelling evidence for the keeping of
livestock by hunter-gatherer groups and their probable incorporation as ancillary resources into their subsistence strategies.

Study description Extraction and analysis of lipid residues from archaeological potsherds from Lesotho to explore hunter-gatherer subsistence patterns
following the arrival of agricultural groups in southern Africa.
Research sample Potsherds from hunter-gatherer contexts from two archaeological sites (Likoaeng and Sehonghong) dating to the mid-late first
millennium AD and from a 19th/20th century agricultural settlement (Mokatlapoli).
Sampling strategy Potsherds from Likoaeng (n=30), Sehonghong (n=17) and Mokatlapoli (n=27) were selected for lipid extraction based on availability
and the size of potsherd. Analysis of lipid extracts was undertaken based on lipid concentration.
Data collection Lipids were extracted from potsherds using published protocols. Lipid residues were characterised using gas-chromatography (GC)
and GC-mass spectrometry (GC-MS). Stable isotope values of individual C16 and C18 fatty acids were determined using GC-c-Isotope
ratio MS (GC-C-IRMS). For C14 dating, C16 and C18 fatty acids were isolated using preparative capillary GC, combusted in an
elemental analyser, graphitised in an automated graphitisation system and measured on a MICADAS accelerator mass spectrometer
(AMS).
Timing and spatial scale Potsherds were excavated/collected in the 1990s. Sampling, lipid extraction and analysis of all samples were carried out between
January-April 2015. A second lipid extraction and radiocarbon dating of LK10 and SHH42 was carried out was carried out in Jan 2019.
Data exclusions No data were excluded from the analysis.
Reproducibility For compound specific stable isotope analysis samples were run in duplicate. Replicate measurements were within analytical
reproducibility of ±0.4‰ for all samples and the final results are an average taken of the two values. Where C14 dates from fatty
acids C16 and C18 agreed within 2 sigma, a weighted mean was taken to produce the final date for the lipid extract. For LIK10, the
first C14 measurements for fatty acids C18 and C16 fell outside 2sigma so the measurements were repeated.
Randomization The study was conducted on potsherds collected from three archaeological sites and the results were grouped according to site so
randomization was not applicable.
Blinding Blinding was not relevant due to the nature of the study.
