6. Verdict

Grade: PARTIAL. The core novel Study 4 headline result — that for branded real advertisements greater category–appeal mismatch is associated with higher evaluation (r=.50) — was independently and exactly re-produced from the paper's published aggregate data, along with its companion correlations and partial correlations. The paper's other headline claims (branding alters attitude functions, and the function-matching advantage disappears at the brand level; Studies 1–3) could not be independently re-run because the raw reproduction data/scripts reside on OSF, which was in sustained emergency maintenance throughout the audit window.

No contradictions were found, and all user-verifiable numbers matched. This is consistent with (but does not independently re-confirm end-to-end) the human FLoRA registry rating of "computationally reproducible" (a computational reproduction by Somo et al. 2020 is recorded in FORRT's FLoRA atlas).
