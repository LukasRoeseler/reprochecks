Savani\_PsychologSci\_2010\_88xa
--------------------------------

This is the single-trace claim selected from this paper in Phase 1. It
includes two levels of claim 3: 3a, a statement of the finding, and 3b,
the specification of the test. In bushel-trace claims, claim 3b is not
coded, and claim 3 is analagous to the single-trace 3a.

-   **Claim 2 (Abstract statement):** \[W\]e found that people in U.S.
    American contexts, where the disjoint model of agency is prevalent,
    are more likely than those in Indian contexts to construe … other
    individuals’ behaviors as choices

-   **Claim 3a (Finding statement):** Americans tend to construe more of
    another actor’s actions as choices than do Indian participants;
    Americans therefore identify more instances in a video as times when
    the actor made a choice.

-   **Claim 3b (Test specification):** The number of button presses made
    by participants in either a control condition (press a button every
    time the actor touches an object with his hands) or the
    construal-of-choice condition (pressing a bottom whenever the actor
    made a choice) was submitted to a 2 (culture) × 2 (condition) ANOVA.

-   **Claim 4 (Results statement):** In Study 3, there was an
    interaction between culture (Indian, American) and condition
    (construal-of-choice vs. control task) in how often participants
    pressed the response button, F(1, 120) = 5.69, p &lt; .02, p\_rep =
    .96

-   **Claim 2 (Abstract statement):** In five studies, we found that
    people in U.S. American contexts, where the disjoint model of agency
    is prevalent, are more likely than those in Indian contexts to
    construe their own and other individuals’ behaviors as choices, to
    construe ongoing behaviors and behaviors recalled from memory as
    choices, to construe naturally occurring and experimentally
    controlled behaviors as choices, to construe mundane and important
    actions as choices, and to construe personal and interpersonal
    actions as choices.

-   **Claim 3 (Finding statement):** We hypothesized that American
    participants would list more actions than Indian participants in the
    choice-listing condition but not in the control condition.

-   **Claim 4 (Result statement):** Upon submitting the number of
    actions listed to a 2 (cultural context) × 2 (condition) analysis of
    variance (ANOVA), we found…a Cultural Context × Condition
    interaction, F(1, 175) = 5.56, p\_rep = .93, p &lt; .02, partial
    eta-squared = .031. Supporting our hypothesis, simple-effects t
    tests revealed that American participants identified significantly
    more choices in the stream of behavior that they engaged in the day
    before than did Indian participants, t(86) = 3.72, p\_rep = .99, p
    &lt; .001, d = 0.80. Notably, the two groups did not differ in the
    number of actions listed in the control condition, t &lt; 1 (see
    Fig. 1), which indicates that Indian participants are unlikely to
    have a general tendency to produce fewer responses than U.S.
    American participants.

-   **Statistical evidence:** F(1, 175) = 5.56, p\_rep = .93, p &lt;
    .02, partial eta-squared = .031; t(86) = 3.72, p\_rep = .99, p &lt;
    .001, d = 0.80; t &lt; 1
