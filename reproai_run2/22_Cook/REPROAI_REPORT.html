1. Data sourcing

The published JPE comment is paywalled and its full text was not retrievable; sourcing relied on (a) the OpenAlex abstract and bibliographic record, and (b) the authors’ own replication material, which reproduces the comment’s tables.

  • Comment replication package (counted as obtained): mirrored on OSF node ekzdg (DATASET_ETHNOATLAS.dta, do file.do, PaperLatLongClimate.dta), originating from the Bank of Canada OAR repository (record 7690). Provides the exact Stata code driving the comment’s Table 1 and robustness tables.
  • I4R DP No. 82 full text (open-access discussion paper) retrieved from the RWI mirror of EconStor (EconStor itself is bot-blocked).
  • Original MMP replication package (Chicago/JPE) itself was not directly retrieved; the comment’s code is built directly on the MMP EthnoAtlas data included in the package above.

Note: EconStor and the Bank of Canada OAR site are protected by (Anubis / AWS-WAF) JavaScript challenges; the same data were reachable via the OSF mirror.

2. Headline numerical claims extracted

  1. Appropriability over productivity (Claim 2 of MMP): conditional on cultivating cereals, land productivity has no significant effect on hierarchy; cereal cultivation itself raises the 1–5 hierarchy index substantially (MMP/replication Table 1, column 4: Cereals ≈ 1.06, p≈0.06; LandProd ≈ −0.04, p≈0.67).
  2. Hierarchy-level scope (comment abstract, claim 1): the evidence for the appropriability theory holds when moving from tribe–chiefdom to state, and not more broadly across the hierarchy scale.
  3. Outlier sensitivity (comment abstract, claim 2): the conclusions are driven by a handful of outliers; statistical significance at the 10% level is lost upon winsorization of ~2.1% (or trimming of ~1.2%) of the cereal-advantage (instrument) distribution.

3. Independent re-computation

All regressions were re-run independently in R (R 4.6.1) using AER::ivreg (2SLS) with cluster-robust standard errors (sandwich, HC1) clustered at the country (iso) level, mirroring Stata ivreg2 ..., cluster(iso). Variables were constructed exactly per the provided do file.do. Sample: non-desertic societies with agricultural data, year ≥ 1500, N=952 (877 for the lasso column).

4. Findings

P2-1 — Minor SE/p discrepancies vs published table. Coefficients reproduce exactly, but cluster-robust SEs/p-values differ marginally from I4R DP Table 1 (e.g., 2SLS+FE p=0.048 vs 0.046; and column 4 CerMain SE 0.560 vs 0.447). Arises from cluster-SE method/procedure differences; does not change any conclusion.
P2-2 — Exact winsorization threshold borderline. At the comment’s stated 2.1% winsorization / 1.2% trimming, my HC1-cluster recomputation yields p=0.090 / 0.094 — nominally still <0.10. Significance is clearly lost by 3%. The qualitative “driven by outliers” claim holds regardless.
P3-1 — I4R DP Table 1, col 6 F-statistic 16.10 vs 16.11 in the published MMP paper (red-text, cosmetic).
P3-2 — I4R DP Table 1, col 5 first-stage coefficient missing decimal place and SE 0.029 vs published 0.068 (red-text, cosmetic).

5. Verdict

Grade: CONSISTENT. The comment’s headline claims were independently re-computed from its own data/code and verified: (1) the appropriability effect is concentrated at the tribe/chiefdom-to-state transitions rather than holding across the whole hierarchy scale; and (2) the theoretically central result is driven by a handful of outlier societies, with 10% significance lost under modest winsorization/trimming. Land productivity shows no significant effect once cereal cultivation is controlled for (Claim 2 of MMP also reproduced).

Matches human code? Yes. The human FLoRA code for I4R DP No. 82 is “computational issues” — i.e. data/code closely reproduce the main Table 1 results with small, non-interpretation-changing discrepancies. My independent recomputation reproduces the coefficients exactly, reproduces both headline qualitative claims, and surfaces only minor SE/p/threshold discrepancies — fully consistent with a “computational issues” classification.
