New quarterly UK narrative "exogenous discretionary tax change" series (1945/1955–2009) built with the Romer–Romer (2010) narrative method, used as the exogenous regressor in a quarterly VAR to estimate the impulse response of GDP (and other aggregates) to tax changes.

Mode: web audit + extraction of the paper and the published human reproduction, with an attempted independent re-execution. The audit therefore verifies the headline numbers via internal-consistency and cross-source checks of the published human reproduction rather than a from-scratch recomputation.

Verdict: headline numbers corroborated but not independently re-computed here; the package is genuinely "computationally reproducible" and I agree with the human FLoRA classification. The headline claim — a 1%-of-GDP tax cut raises UK GDP by ≈0.6% on impact, rising to a ≈2.5% peak over about three years — is stated in the abstract, reproduced exactly by the published human reproduction (I4R DP132): author-code impact 0.599, peak 2.458 (Stata replication 0.567 / 2.226). My own numeric checks confirm the human reproduction's numbers are internally consistent (z = coef/SE and 95% CIs re-derive within rounding). I was, however, unable to obtain the raw replication data (ICPSR download wall), so I could not perform an independent from-scratch VAR impulse-response recomputation; for that reason the headline figures are marked corroborated / not independently verified (CLO-001, P2). Two further P2/P3 findings come from the human reproduction: the author's original MATLAB bootstrap samples residuals not jointly across the K endogenous variables, inflating the precision of the peak response (P2); and the headline p-values are not emitted by the author code but were reconstructed as a bootstrap share-below-zero (effectively one-sided) (P3).

Findings (for the authors / readers)

P2 — medium

CLO-001 — Raw replication data are effectively inaccessible for automated third-party verification. The only distribution channel for data.xlsx/*.m/table1.wf1 is the AEA→ICPSR deposit (10.3886/E112651V1). OpenICPSR serves its /download routes behind a Cloudflare managed challenge (blocks even a real headless Chromium), and the new ICPSR deposit host redirects anonymous downloads to a Keycloak login. No third-party mirror exists (Zenodo, GitHub, Wayback CDX all empty). As a consequence a reader cannot programmatically retrieve the data, and any independent recomputation of the headline VAR impulse responses is blocked at the access layer regardless of the code's quality. This is an accessibility finding (not a numbers problem): it prevents independent verification in this audit. Fix suggestion: mirror the package to a freely downloadable channel (e.g., the AEA site, an OSF/Zenodo record).

CLO-002 — The author's original MATLAB bootstrap samples residuals non-jointly across the VAR block, understating the peak-response standard error. Identified by the human reproduction (I4R DP132 §2.1): the original bootstrap routine does not draw residuals jointly for all K endogenous variables, so it does not preserve contemporaneous cross-equation correlation. Resampling jointly raises the peak-response standard error (0.797 → 1.012) and changes whether the impact effect clears 95% significance. The point estimates (0.599 / 2.458) are unchanged; the headline story is unaffected, but reported standard errors/significance depend on this bootstrap choice and should be documented. Fix: adopt joint residual resampling (as the standard var irf bootstrap does) and re-report SEs.

P3 — low

CLO-003 — Headline p-values are not emitted by the replication code and were reconstructed (bootstrap share-below-zero, effectively one-sided). Reproducers initially could not match the in-text p-values because the author code produces only coefficients/bootstrap distributions, not p-values; matching required interpreting them as the share of bootstrap replications below zero. Minor documentation gap. Fix: emit and report the p-value construction in the code/output.

CLO-004 — Reproduction requires proprietary software (MATLAB and EViews). The package mixes MATLAB (Figures3to9.m, Table2.m) with an EViews workfile (table1.wf1), so faithful re-execution is not possible in open-source-only tooling (in contrast, the I4R team re-implemented the baseline in Stata). Not a correctness issue. Fix: port core modules to an open, reproducible language (R/Python/Stata) or document versions.

What this audit does not guarantee

  • No from-scratch recomputation of the headline impulse responses. Because the raw series could not be retrieved (access wall, CLO-001), the headline numbers (C1–C3) are corroborated via the published human reproduction and internal-consistency checks — but were not independently recomputed by this audit. If the data were made downloadable, the VAR IRF could be regenerated end-to-end (specification is fully documented in I4R §2 and the Online Appendix).
  • The I4R DP132 report was obtained through the Wayback Machine mirror (EconStor original is behind the "Anubis" anti-scraper proof-of-work); the report's internal numbers were sanity-checked as above and are self-consistent.
  • Full paper text is paywalled; headline claims were taken from the peer-reviewed abstract and corroborated by the supplement and the I4R summary, which quote the same figures.

Agreement with human FLoRA classification

The human classification was "computationally reproducible". I agree. The package (code + data) is complete and self-contained; a published, independent reproduction (I4R DP132) states it reproduced all VAR tables and figures exactly using the author code, and my internal-consistency checks confirm their reproduced numbers are coherent and match the paper's abstract. My inability to recompute here is purely an access-layer limitation (data not machine-downloadable), not an indication of any defect in the package or the paper's headline results.
