3. Verification (independently re-computed)

Reproduced the parsimonious dose-response model (equation 1; temperature bins <40°F, 80–89°F, >90°F), using first-difference + lag specification with year-month fixed effects, state-month fixed effects and state-month-specific quadratic time trends, population-weighted (analytic) with standard errors clustered at the state. In this parameterization the lag-level coefficient equals the reported “cumulative dynamic estimate.”

5. Verdict vs human FLoRA code

The registry marks this study (flora) as “computational issues”. The OSF human reproduction transparency trail (TransparencyTrail.txt) states that each tested claim “did reproduce” (matching values, e.g. >90°F = 0.93%), but notes the reruns are computationally heavy (Stata, maxvar 12000, ~1–2 hours per table), which is the basis of the “computational issues”/repro_possible=No registry flag. My independent Python reproduction confirms the central claims reproduce (0.93% / 2.2% / 0.34% for the >90°F effect). The verdict on the central dose–response is therefore reproducible, matching the human trail, while the computational-issues label remains appropriate as a practical difficulty rather than a numerical failure. Grade is CONSISTENT (central dose-response reproduced, conclusions hold), not FULL, because the abstract welfare claims (14,000 fatalities, $83–$186 B surplus) and the AC-mechanism coefficient were not independently re-derived within this audit.
