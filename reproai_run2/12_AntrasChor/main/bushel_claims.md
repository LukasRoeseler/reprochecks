Antra\_s\_Econometrica\_2013\_a2Yx
----------------------------------

This is the single-trace claim selected from this paper in Phase 1. It
includes two levels of claim 3: 3a, a statement of the finding, and 3b,
the specification of the test. In bushel-trace claims, claim 3b is not
coded, and claim 3 is analagous to the single-trace 3a.

-   **Claim 2 (Abstract statement):** Combining data from the U.S.
    Census Bureau’s Related Party Trade database and estimates of U.S.
    import demand elasticities from Broda and Weinstein (2006), we find
    empirical evidence broadly supportive of our key predictions.

-   **Claim 3a (Finding statement):** The authors uncover a positive and
    statistically significant relationship between each of the measures
    of downstreamness and the intrafirm import share in a given sector,
    with this relationship emerging only for high values of the demand
    elasticity faced by buyer industries (i.e., in the complement case).

-   **Claim 3b (Test specification):** The novel predictions from the
    authors’ model regarding the role of downstreamness are tested in
    Table III \[Dependent Variable: Intrafirm Import Share; Estimation
    is by OLS\] using the DUse\_TUse \[ratio of the aggregate direct use
    to the aggregate total use\] measure of production line position.
    Following the guidance of the authors’ theoretical model, they run
    their benchmark specification from equation (32) in column 2, which
    includes the interactions of DUse\_TUse with their proxies for the
    sequential substitutes (Elas &lt; Median) and complements (Elas &gt;
    Median) cases, as well as a dummy variable for the sequential
    complements case. The term selected is ‘DUse\_TUse × 1(Elas &gt;
    Median), beta\_2.’

-   **Claim 4 (Results statement):** The effect of downstreamness is
    positive and significant at the 5% level when the average buyer
    demand elasticity is above the median for this variable
    (beta\_2 &gt; 0), consistent with a greater propensity toward the
    integration of input suppliers that enter further downstream in the
    value chain (from Table III, column (2), ‘DUse\_TUse × 1(Elas &gt;
    Median), beta\_2’ term: estimate = 0.171 \[0.067\]).
