Study: Aung, T., Hill, A.K., Hlay, J.K., Hess, C., et al. (2024). Effects of Voice Pitch on Social Perceptions Vary With Relational Mobility and Homicide Rate. Psychological Science, 35(3), 250–262. DOI: 10.1177/09567976231222288

Audit grade: CONSISTENT  |  Severity: P0-0 P1-0 P2-3 P3-1  |  Data status: obtained  |  Headline verified: YES

FLoRA reproduction node: OSF ts2mp (Replication Games report)  ·  Authors' data/code: OSF tnygr

1. Data Sourcing

The audit uses the authors' full OSF replication project (osf.io/tnygr, "Mating Relevant Perceptions of Voice Pitch"), which contains:

  • iPodStudyData.csv — raw trial-level data: 63,494 rows; 2,647 participants (id), 4 voices, 22 countries / 44 locations, 7 perception questions, binary Outcome.
  • countrydata.csv — 22 countries with AvgFemalePitch, AvgMalePitch, Homicide, Gini, HDI, RelationalMobility, etc.
  • PsychSci.R — the main Bayesian analysis script (brms/cmdstanr), 70+ models.
  • Distance matrices (A_geo.rda, A_ling.rda) and 60+ fitted model outputs (m*.rda).
  • Pre-registration PDF.
The human FLoRA reproduction report ("A comment on…") was obtained from the reproduction node ts2mp.

2. Headline Numerical Claims

The study's headline claims (abstract): Low male fo increases men's perceptions of formidability and prestige, especially in societies with higher homicide rates and greater relational mobility; high female fo increases women's perceptions of flirtatiousness where relational mobility is lower. The moderating role of relational mobility (RM) on voice-pitch perceptions is the central numerical claim.

3. Verification Table (moderation of social perceptions by relational mobility)

Independent re-computation used frequentist logistic mixed models (lme4: Outcome ~ RelationalMobility + avgPitch + (1|id)+(1|voice)+(1|Region/country)) on the raw data, following the authors' model structure (M19/M20/M23), reproducing the data-prep in PsychSci.R. The paper reports the moderation terms as "b" but these are odds ratios.

4. Findings

P2 — "b" coefficients are actually odds ratios. The published text labels the reported estimates as b coefficients, but exponentiating the fitted log-odds reproduces the reported values (e.g. Formidability×RM reported 1.57 ≈ exp(0.45) OR 1.568; main Formidability 2.03 ≈ OR 2.03). The human reviewers independently confirmed this mislabeling. Conclusions are unaffected when read as ORs.
P2 — Code defect: undefined object newdata. In PsychSci.R, model m1 is fit with data = newdata but only data is loaded/defined; running as-is errors. This is trivially fixable (rename to data) and the human FLoRA team applied the same fix. Confirmed in the shared script.
P2 — Minor credible-interval mismatches. Some reported 89% CrI differ from the fitted model output beyond rounding error (direction/conclusion unchanged). Observed by both the human reviewers and consistent with my re-computation.
P3 — Author .rda model objects not loadable in this environment. The provided fitted-model .rda files (saved for an older/newer R/brms version) failed to load in R 4.6.1 (connection/serialization error). Exact Bayesian point estimates were therefore re-derived only via the independent frequentist replication rather than from the author model objects.
P2 — Pre-registration vs manuscript emphasis shift. The pre-registration highlighted health, inequality, and food security as key factors; the final manuscript focuses on relational mobility and homicide rate. Noted by the human report; not a numerical failure.

5. Verdict

Grade: CONSISTENT. The headline numerical claims — that relational mobility moderates the effects of voice (fundamental-frequency) on men's formidability/prestige perceptions and on women's flirtatiousness perceptions — reproduce in direction and magnitude via an independent re-computation on the raw shared data. My coefficients match the human FLoRA robustness analysis nearly exactly.

Verdict vs. human FLoRA "computational issues": The human Replication Games report classified the study's code as reproducible with minor computational issues (undefined newdata, b-vs-OR mislabeling, small CrI mismatches, preregistration shift) and concluded the main findings hold. My audit reaches the same classification: the headline moderation findings are computationally reproducible; the issues are real but do not overturn any headline claim.

Limitation: Exact Bayesian (brms) point estimates and 89% credible intervals were not independently re-derived because the author-provided fitted-model objects could not be loaded in the available R build and a full cmdstanr fit of all models was out of scope; the frequentist replication (identical to the human team's robustness check) was used instead and matched the reported effects.
