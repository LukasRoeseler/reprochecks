An empirically sound and almost perfectly reproducible replication. Every pre-registered statistical result the manuscript reports reproduces from the shipped processed data to the manuscript's own precision — the behavioral accuracy table (Table 1), all 12 grammaticality × electrode ANOVAs (Table 2), both difference-wave ANOVAs (Table 3), all 8 Bayes factors (Table 4), and the appendix RATN three-way ANOVA — verified independently in R and cross-checked in Python (SS-decomposition, separate code path). The headline conclusion — a P600 component for both syntactic (language) and harmonic (music) violations, with statistically indistinguishable amplitude and scalp distribution — is robustly reproduced on N=35 (>2× the original N=15). Findings are minor and do not threaten the conclusions: (a) the two appendix RATN Bayes-factor magnitudes are ~3%/1.5% below the reported values (Monte-Carlo sampling noise in BayesFactor — the author's own rendered notebook gives a third value, 430,597 ±6.69%, confirming the several-percent noise); and (b) a genuine, previously-missed sample-consistency defect — behavioral Table 1 was computed over N=36 because the author's exclusion filter fails to remove excluded subject 8 (zero-padded ID "08" never matches integer 8), so the behavioral analysis sample differs from the stated analytic N=35. Numeric impact is negligible (~0.1–0.2 percentage points) and no conclusion changes.

Findings (for the authors)

P2 — medium

ADV-001 — The two appendix RATN Bayes-factor magnitudes do not reproduce exactly (~3% / 1.5% low). The appendix states the data are "431,034 times less likely" under the full three-way RATN model than the participant-only null, and "39 times less likely" under the full model than the no-three-way model. Re-running the identical BayesFactor::anovaBF (same 18-model list, same data, same seed 12604, 1/bf_full) gives 418,304× (3.0% below the manuscript). Notably, the author's own rendered notebook (analysis.nb.html, 1/bf.anova.RATN[18]) produced a third, different value, 430,597 ±6.69% — not the 431,034 printed in the manuscript and not my 418,304. Three independent draws of the same model/data (paper 431,034; notebook 430,597; this audit 418,304) differing by up to ~3–6% is textbook Monte-Carlo sampling noise, and the notebook's own ±6.69% error tag shows the estimate is only trustworthy to roughly ±7% at this magnitude. Method and code are identical; the print-to-print gap is pure MC variance, amplified by the exponential sensitivity of large BFs. The qualitative conclusion (overwhelming support against the RATN three-way interaction) is unaffected. Fix: report these two appendix BFs to two significant figures (4.2e5, ~38) or annotate the ~2–3% MC error; the deterministic ANOVA (F(4,136)=.366, p=.832) already carries the same conclusion.

ADV-003 — Behavioral Table 1 was computed over N=36 — including excluded subject 8 — not the stated analytic N=35. The author's exclusion filter is filter(!subject_id %in% c(8,21,11,23,25,40)), but subject_id in beh_data_tidy.csv is a zero-padded character string ("08", "11", …). R coerces the integer 8 to "8", which never equals "08"; only the two-digit IDs (11, 21, 23, 25, 40) are removed. Verified: the author-style filter removes {11,21,23,25,40} and keeps subject 8 (N=36); a proper integer filter removes all 6 (N=35). The manuscript's Table 1 values (93.3/88.2/84.5/69.1) match the 36-subject computation exactly, confirming the reported numbers were produced with excluded subject 8 still in the behavioral sample. The intended N=35 values are 93.43/88.19/84.29/69.29 — a ~0.1–0.2 percentage-point difference that changes nothing qualitatively (accuracy still "consistent with Patel"). Because this is a genuine sample-consistency/provenance defect in the reproducibility code where the behavioral sample differs from the EEG sample and from the Methods' stated exclusion rules, it is rated P2. Fix: coerce IDs to integer (as.integer(subject_id) %in% c(8,21,11,23,25,40)) or store IDs without leading zeros, then recompute Table 1 on N=35; add a check that the behavioral and EEG samples match.

P3 — low

ADV-002 — The appendix "39 times" three-way interaction ratio is 37.77 (1.5% off). Same Monte-Carlo source as ADV-001; direction (strongly favors dropping the three-way term) unchanged. Fix: round to ~38 or annotate MC error.

ADV-004 — The RATN/N350 analysis was not pre-registered (disclosed). The appendix transparently states it was added "in response to a reviewer's request." It is an exploratory add-on whose conclusion (no RATN) is robust and which does not affect the pre-registered headline P600 finding. Fix: label it "exploratory / reviewer-requested post hoc" in the main text.

What this audit does not guarantee

  • R-version gap.** The paper's analyses were run in R 3.4.2 with 2017-era packages; this audit reproduced every statistic in R 4.6.1 with current ez 4.5.0 and BayesFactor 0.9.12.4.8. The fact that all closed-form ANOVAs reproduce exactly across a ~9-year version gap confirms those results are version-independent; only the seed-fixed Monte-Carlo Bayes factors carry draw-to-draw noise (unchanged, see above).
  • Processed (post-artifact-rejection) data, not raw EEG. The shipped eeg_data_tidy.csv is the post-NetStation, averaged waveform data the author actually analyzed. The raw .fdt recordings and the NetStation filtering/segmentation/artifact-rejection/average-referencing/baseline-correction pipeline (net triggering) were not re-run. Reproducibility is established from the analysis-ready data — exactly the level at which the Open Data badge is claimed. The exclusion bookkeeping (cutoff 19; S8,S21 tech; S11,S23,S25,S40 low-segment) is internally consistent and matches the manuscript's N=35 for the EEG analyses.
  • Bayes factors are Monte-Carlo estimates. All Table 4 BFs reproduce within the stated ±2% MC error band; only the two very large appendix RATN BFs differ by ~3%/1.5% — and the author's own notebook's render differs from the manuscript's printed value by a comparable ~0.1–3% (430,597 ±6.69% vs 431,034), confirming these magnitudes carry several-percent MC error. Directional and significance conclusions are identical.
  • No pixel-verification of figures. Figures 1–3 (grand-average and difference-wave raster waveform plots) and the electrode-specific O1/O2 observation (C46) were not image-checked (no vision input); the statistical quantities underpinning them were verified numerically (ADV-005, scope only — no action needed).
  • Cross-language ANOVA check used SS decomposition. python_crosscheck.py independently recomputed the within-subjects ANOVA F/p via SS decomposition in a second language and matched every cell of Tables 2–3 in R; this is a code-path independence check, not a live re-run of a proprietary tool.
  • The behavioral Table 1 sample nuance (ADV-003) is quantified and characterized but the manuscript was not re-rendered with the corrected N=35 filter; that is offered as a next step for the authors.
