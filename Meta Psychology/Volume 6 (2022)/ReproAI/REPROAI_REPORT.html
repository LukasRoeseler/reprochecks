Reproduced. The article's closed-form FDR definition (Eq. 1) and its central claim - that for reducing the FDR, decreasing alpha is usually more efficient than increasing statistical power - reproduce exactly from the paper's own "Royal Tea Tasting Society" running example.

Reproduction detail

Formula. FDR = P(H0)·α / [P(H0)·α + (1−P(H0))·power] (Eq. 1).

Running example. P(H0) = 0.80 (four-fifths lack the ability), P(H1) = 0.20, α = 0.05, power = 0.19. False positives = 0.80·0.05 = 0.04; true positives = 0.20·0.19 = 0.038; FDR = 0.04/(0.04+0.038) = 0.5128 (51.3%).

Central claim. Holding the example parameters, decreasing α to 0.01 (power fixed at 0.19) yields FDR = 0.1739 (17.4%), whereas increasing power to 0.80 (α fixed at 0.05) yields FDR = 0.20 (20.0%). Decreasing α achieves a lower FDR, confirming the paper's conclusion that reducing α is usually the more efficient route.

Advisory items

  • ADV-001 (P3). The running example is illustrative; the article's quantitative conclusion follows analytically from Eq. 1. The general claim is verified for this parameterisation, but the full article also relies on a review of 106 prior articles (a literature claim, not a numerical one).
