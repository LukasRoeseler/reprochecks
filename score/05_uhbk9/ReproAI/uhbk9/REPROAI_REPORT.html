Findings (for the authors)

P1 — high

ADV-001 — Study 1's headline veracity×condition interaction statistic is not reproducible from the shipped data. The manuscript reports β=−0.126, F(1,25586)=42.24, p<.0001. Re-running the exact two-way cluster-robust regression on the shipped CSV yields β=−0.130 under the standardized-predictor convention (raw −0.520 under the manuscript's ±0.5 contrast coding) and F(1,25586)=43.70. This F was computed two independent ways (hand-rolled two-way VCV and fixest) that agree; with 853×30=25590 observations and df2=25586 exactly matching the manuscript, the ~3.5% gap is not a rounding or (here, <0.01%) small-sample-correction artifact — it is a data-level difference. The associated simple effects also differ (accuracy 52.75 vs 42.24; sharing 7.39 vs 6.74; d 0.682/0.126 vs 0.657/0.121). Direction and significance are identical (all p<.0001), so no conclusion changes, but the headline numbers on the record cannot be reproduced from what was shipped. Fix: re-run Study 1's cluster2 on the exact analysis set underlying Tables S2/S3; identify whether the CSV or the manuscript is authoritative and reconcile both (report exact F/β/df with a reproducible, version-selecting preprocessing pipeline — see ADV-006).

P2 — medium

ADV-002 — The printed accuracy simple-effect F equals the interaction F and does not reproduce. The Results state the accuracy simple effect as F(1,25586)=42.24 and the interaction as F(1,25586)=42.24 — identical. These are different linear combinations and are not guaranteed equal; the reimplementation gives accuracy-simple F=52.75 (interaction F=43.70). The duplicated value is consistent with a copy/paste from the interaction line. Fix: correct the accuracy (and re-verify sharing) simple-effect F to the value actually obtained on the author's analysis set.

ADV-003 — "32.4% more people were willing to share [false headlines] than rated them as accurate" does not reproduce. Data: mean share-false = 0.4342, mean accuracy-false = 0.3305 → +10.37 percentage points, i.e. +31.4% relative — not 32.4 pp nor 32.4%. Fix: recompute from the analysis dataset and correct the prose figure (or the dataset).

ADV-004 — Study 2 gender counts are internally inconsistent. The manuscript's "385 males, 468 females, and 8 other/prefer not to answer" sums to 861, not the reported N=856. The shipped data (Gender.0) give 385 M / 463 F / 8 other, which sums correctly to 856; the 468 F figure is off by five. Fix: re-tally gender from the analysis dataset so the demographic components sum to N.

P3 — low

ADV-005 — Study 2 reported interaction β=0.039 vs reimplementation 0.0343. The inferential statistic reproduces exactly (F=17.881, df 25623, p<.0001); the β implies a slightly different DV scaling convention. Document or restate the β to match the rescaled-DV estimate. No conclusion impact.

ADV-006 — Hygiene: order-block selection is load-bearing and the manuscript's links are placeholders. The prepopulated tree's earlier Study-1 script read non-version-selected rating columns and produced a rank-deficient model / NA tables; correct Study 1 pre-processing requires selecting the per-subject version column by Condition (1→base, 2→.0, 3→.1, 4→.2). The manuscript cites data/materials/preregistration as "(link)" placeholders with no working URL. Ship completed runnable scripts and replace placeholder links with the OSF DOI/project id.

What this package already does right (honest positives)

  • Study 2 reproduces cleanly — interaction F, both simple-effect d's, the 2.8× claim, the df2=25623, and Figure 3's r(28) all emerge from the shipped data. This is the strongest positive result and independently validates the shared analysis machinery (identical method in Study 1).
  • Study 1 cell means / Figure 1 reproduce to the published proportions (Accuracy 0.331/0.653; Sharing 0.434/0.497).
  • Study 1 Table 1 simple-effect betas reproduce within ~0.008 across all five individual-difference panels.
  • Demographics (age, gender) for Study 1 reproduce; Study 2 df and sample structure reproduce exactly.
  • A real data + code package was shipped (unlike many preprints), and both studies analyzed at the rating level with two-way clustered SEs, materially reducing the risk of anti-conservative standard errors.

What this audit does not guarantee

  • No Stata execution. Stata was not installed on the host; the shipped .do files and cluster2 were re-implemented in R and cross-checked with fixest. A byte-level Stata run was not performed. The Study 1 F discrepancy (43.70 vs 42.24) persists under two independent R implementations with identical N/df, so it is reported as data-level; a genuine Stata run on the author's analysis set remains the strongest possible adjudication.
  • Scale reliabilities (α) and Study 2 moderation p-values were not independently recomputed (no scoring keys; moderation outside headline scope) — marked ➖ with reasons.
  • Cited background numbers (e.g., effect sizes and claims quoted from prior Pennycook/Rand work in the Discussion) were not traced to their source papers' raw data.
  • Robustness tables S3/S4/S6/S7 (headline exclusions, multilevel models) were not fully re-run; Table S2 col 1 and Table S5 col 1 were spot-checked (Study 2 matches; Study 1 col 1 ≈ −0.252 vs −0.260).
