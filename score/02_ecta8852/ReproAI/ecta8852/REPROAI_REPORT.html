Substantially reproducible on the raw data. The paper's two central claims reconstruct cleanly from the shipped per-period data: (1) the no-communication strategic-voting comparative statics (Table III) reproduce near-exactly across all preference treatments and voting rules, including the type-level cells and wrong-jury-outcome rows; and (2) the outcome-frequency tables (Table V exactly; Table VI point estimates) reconstruct, supporting the headline that deliberation erases most institutional differences. The total sample N=549 reproduces exactly, and the paper's flagship individual-level probit result — the homogeneous no-communication Red sample marginal effect — reproduces exactly (0.814) once the at-means marginal-effect convention is adopted. The findings are clustered in data-integrity and documentation: ADV-001 (P1) a shipped raw file (PART_9_36_chat.txt) contains duplicated per-subject rows (periods 1–5), so a naive recompute of partisan r=9 with-communication majority-red gives 40% vs the printed 84.4% (≈81.8% after de-duplication); ADV-002 (P2) HOM_7_36_chat.txt is shipped yet unused (the communication homogeneous r=7 cell matches only the 27-subject session, and Table I Footnote b says only no-chat ran there); ADV-003 (P2) Table VI's 95% CIs do not reproduce under the stated normal approximation (printed ones are narrower, consistent with an individual-level N that ignores within-group clustering); and three P3 documentation issues (payoff averages $9.34/$13.43 vs $9.53/$13.11; fn16 decision-time seconds; secondary probit cells). Message-content tables (VII, VIII) remain not independently reproducible without the coders' rubric.

Findings (for the authors)

P1 — high

ADV-001 — PART_9_36_chat.txt contains duplicated per-subject records in periods 1–5. The file holds 540 rows over only 9 periods: periods 1–5 carry 72 rows each (4 groups × 18 rows = each of the 9 subjects recorded twice), periods 6–9 carry 36 rows (9 rows/group, correct). The expected row count for 36 subjects × 9 rounds is 324, so ≈180 duplicate records are present. On the shipped file as-is, the partisan r=9 with-communication majority-red cell recomputes to 40.0% (paper 84.4%); de-duplicating by (period, group, subject) yields 81.8% (paper 84.4%). This is the only file with such duplication (all other segments are clean), and it is exactly the one treatment whose Table VI point I could not reproduce naively. The paper's printed value is consistent with the cleaned data, confirming the authors' analysis used a file not identical to the one shipped. Fix: redistribute a de-duplicated PART_9_36_chat.txt and state in the codebook that its periods 1–5 contain duplicate records.

P2 — medium

ADV-002 — HOM_7_36_chat.txt is shipped but unused; N contradicts Footnote b. Paper Table III (and Table V) with-communication homogeneous r=7 reports N=486 individual decisions (54 group decisions), which matches only HOM_7_27_chat.txt (27 subjects × 18 rounds). The shipped package also contains HOM_7_36_chat.txt (36 subjects, 540 decisions); including it gives 1026 decisions / 114 groups for that cell. Table I Footnote b states the 36- and 45-subject homogeneous r=7 sessions were "no-chat only", yet a chat file for the 36-subject session was shipped. A reader who naively loads every shipped file cannot reproduce the printed communication panel. Fix: document the exact session set entering each communication cell and reconcile Footnote b with the shipped files.

ADV-003 — Table VI 95% CIs do not reproduce under the stated "normal approximation". All Table VI point estimates reproduce, but the printed CIs are materially narrower than Wald group-level CIs. Example: homogeneous r=7 majority-red 92.6% — printed [89.3,95.9], recomputed Wald at the reported group-count (n=27) [82.7,100]; homogeneous r=9 majority-red 94.7% — printed [92.4,97.1], recomputed [87.6,100]. The printed widths imply an effective N of roughly 9× the group count (≈ individual-decision level), i.e. within-group clustering is ignored and uncertainty is understated. Fix: disclose the CI unit/N and report group-level (cluster-robust) intervals, or correct the intervals.

P3 — low

ADV-004 — average payoffs do not reproduce. Per-subject recompute: no-chat $9.34 (paper $9.53), chat $13.43 (paper $13.11). Both are ≈2% off in opposite directions and the gaps persist restricting to full-15-round sessions ($10.07/$13.45). The averaging rule is undocumented. Fix: state the payoff-averaging rule (subsample, practice rounds, per-session weighting) or correct the figures.

ADV-005 — fn16 decision-time absolute seconds do not reproduce. Recomputed no-communication col-10 (seconds) means are 8.5/11.9/13.2 (with-signal) and 11.8/14.9/16.7 (against-signal), vs the printed 41.4/55.1/30.5 and 51.3/72.2/36.7. The paper's substantive claim — "subjects took at least 20% longer when voting against their signal" — does reproduce (hom +39%, het +25%, part +27%). The footnote seconds reconstruct from no shipped variable. Fix: clarify the decision-time measure/subsample or correct the printed seconds.

ADV-006 — Table IV secondary probit cells not reproduced at printed precision. The flagship homogeneous no-communication Red sample marginal effect reproduces exactly (= 0.814) under the at-means (MEM) convention, and Rule7/Rule9 are close (0.259/0.424 vs 0.271/0.385). The communication column (MEM red sample 0.579 vs 0.504) and the heterogeneous/partisan columns do not reconstruct at the 3-decimal precision shown, most likely due to undocumented covariate/lag definitions and per-column marginal-effect handling. Fix: archive the estimation spec and state the marginal-effect convention, lag, and "late" definitions.

What the package already does right (honest positives)

  • Raw per-period behavior data are shipped and machine-readable with an explicit codebook (File_Label_Key.docx, BasicParamOverview.docx) — no opaque summary-only releases.
  • The central no-communication strategic-voting results reconstruct near-exactly from the raw data across all three preference treatments, three rules, and the type-level cells — a strong, directly verifiable core.
  • Table V (outcome by number of red signals) reconstructs exactly for both communication regimes, and the with/without-communication outcome differential that drives the paper's main conclusion reproduces.
  • The total sample N=549 reconstructs exactly, and the flagship individual-level probit coefficient reproduces exactly.

What this audit does not guarantee

  • No author code was run. The archive contains no analysis script (despite the "data and programs" name); all statistics were independently recomputed in R from the raw .txt. A genuine Stata/limdep re-run of Table IV was not attempted and is not byte-for-byte reproducible without the spec.
  • Message-content tables (Table VII, VIII) and the Wilcoxon tests require the research assistants' manual Truthful/Lie/Nothing and signal-message categorization. The .xls message logs are shipped, but without the coding rubric these cells are not independently reproducible (➖).
  • The Table III with-communication x%/y% cells require a full-signal-profile optimal-decision normalization that was not re-implemented end-to-end (➖ with method; the underlying N's are reported).
  • Demographic/design parameters without a data footprint (e.g. the $5 show-up fee, recruitment) were not machine-verified.
  • CIs in Table VI could not be matched to their exact printed method; the recomputed Wald intervals and the likely individual-level-N cause are offered as the evidence, not a claim to have found the authors' exact function.
