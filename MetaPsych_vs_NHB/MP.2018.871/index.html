This is a very clean stochastic-simulation reproduction. Every load-bearing statistic reproduces. Re-running the author's own simulation code in R 4.6.1 reproduces every primary AUC the manuscript reports: Experiment 1 (aucp = .959/.96/.039 vs manuscript .96/.97/.04), the power series 50–99% (all five points match mean/median/SD), Experiments 2 (.992/1/.014 vs .99/1/.01), 3 (.973/.99/.035 vs .97/.99/.04), 7 (.959/.96/.039, identical for all three prior odds) and 8 (.954/.984/.060 vs .95/.97/.07). The paper's central, load-bearing claim — that the AUC for p-values and the AUC for Bayes factors are equal (perfectly corresponding ROCs) — reproduces exactly (root-mean-square difference = 0; the two AUC vectors are bit-identical, because log-p and log-BF are strictly monotone functions of the same t-statistic). The effect-size claims also hold exactly: signed Cohen's-d AUC (.976) ≥ p AUC (.959), and absolute-d AUC equals p AUC to the last decimal (.9592 = .9592, by construction). The optional-stopping (p-hacking) experiment reproduces its qualitative claims: p-hacking raised the hit rate by ≈28% (recomp +27.9 pp: .471 → .750) while raising the false-alarm rate by ≈12% (recomp +10.6 pp: .044 → .149); at high power (>99%) hit rate → ~100% with a ≈5-point false-alarm increase; and the hack frequency of 4.3 per set of 20 studies reproduces under the author's own 10-null + 10-effect default (recomp 3.97, SD 1.73, range 1–8). The 6 findings are documentation/hygiene items, none of which changes a conclusion. The one medium item (ADV-001, P2) is a parameter-naming/documentation hazard in the optional-stopping script: its numStudies argument is applied per effect size, so a reader who reads the name literally ("number of studies") and sets it to 20 gets 40 studies and a doubled hack count (8.66); the manuscript's 4.3 reproduces only under the 10+10 convention, which the author's shipped default (numStudies = 10) happens to satisfy. Five P3 items concern the missing seed and an internal median inconsistency (ADV-002), a code-vs-text loop-count wording (ADV-003), a test-type-agnostic Bayes factor (ADV-004), the absence of shipped raw simulated data (ADV-005), and an overstated prose crossover claim (ADV-006).

Findings (for the authors)

P2 — medium

ADV-001 — The optional-stopping script's numStudies argument is applied per effect size, creating a design/documentation ambiguity. In Witt_SDT_Simulations_OptionalStopping_v2.R, runSims takes a single numStudies and loops for (j in effectSizes) with effectSizes = c(0, .5), so numStudies is run for each condition. The manuscript describes "sets of 20 studies, half null / half effect" = 10 + 10 = 20, and the author's shipped default (numStudies <- 10) correctly yields 20 studies and reproduces the reported hack count C22 (this audit: mean 3.97, SD 1.73, range 1–8 vs manuscript 4.3, SD 2, range 0–11). However, a reader who takes the variable's literal name ("number of studies") as the total and sets numStudies = 20 obtains 40 studies and a ~2× hack count (this audit: 8.66). The shipped default is consistent with the manuscript, so this does not change any conclusion; it is an under-documented parameter that can silently double the simulated sample and obscure the reported 4.3. Fix: rename to numPerCondition or document "per effect-size; set to 10 for 10+10 = 20 studies total," and ship the seed (see ADV-002).

P3 — low

ADV-002 — No random seed reported; single-seed AUCs drift; an internal median inconsistency exists across experiments. Re-running the author's code with seeds 1–5 gives Exp-1 AUCp means .959 / .947 / .954 / .953 / .953 (pooled .953; median rock-stable at .96). The manuscript additionally prints different descriptive values for identical simulation conditions: Exp1 p-AUC median .97 (C6) vs Exp7 (same underlying simulation) p-AUC median .98 (C35), and n=105 reported as AUCp .99 in Experiment 2 but .984 in the power-series row for 95% power (also n=105). My recompute gives .96 (Exp1/Exp7) and .992 (n=105). The published values fall within the drift band but cannot be recovered to the last decimal without the seed. Fix: report the seed(s), quote the pooled/median AUC as the primary statistic, and align the Exp1/Exp7 printed medians.

ADV-003 — Optional-stopping loop-bound wording: "repeated up to 9 more times" (text) vs numTimesRunExtra = 10 (code). The Methods say the process was "repeated up to 9 more times" after the first addition (i.e., 10 additions total); the code uses numTimesRunExtra <- 10 (10 addition loops). Read as "1 first + 9 more = 10," the two agree; read literally the text implies 9 extra additions and the code 10. No effect on results; matters only as code–text alignment. Fix: rephrase to "up to 9 more times (10 additions total)" or set the code constant to match the text.

ADV-004 — The several-tests script computes the Bayes factor as a two-sample t-test for every test type. In Witt_SDT_Simulations_SeveralTests_v2.R, the BF is always exp(ttest.tstat(t = a$statistic, n1 = N, n2 = N, rscale = 0.707)) whether the underlying test is a one-sample t-test, a correlation, or an uneven two-sample t-test. This test-type-agnostic BF is precisely what makes the cross-test AUCs "identical" (C33 / Figure 8). The equal-discriminability result holds by construction, not as an empirical surprise. Fix: add a Methods note that the BF is evaluated with the two-sample ttest.tstat (rscale = 0.707) for all test types.

ADV-005 — No raw simulated data are shipped (only R scripts); exact reproduction requires re-simulation. Because the study is a stochastic simulation and "open data: not applicable," only the five R scripts are shipped; there is no saved data frame of the simulated p-values/BFs. Since no seed is reported (ADV-002), byte-for-byte reproduction is limited to re-simulating. Inherent to the design but worth documenting. Fix (optional): ship the final per-study simulated data frames, or the seed plus a one-command re-run script.

ADV-006 — Prose claim C40b ("as sample size increases p<.005 and all tested BF thresholds outperform p<.10") overstates the crossover. Distance-to-perfection by criterion: at n=105, p<.10 still beats p<.005 (0.086 vs 0.196) and beats BF>2/3/10 (0.108/0.123/0.261); only BF>1 (0.075) beats p<.10. The claimed reversal holds only by n=148 (p<.005 = 0.078 < p<.10 = 0.104). The direction is real, but the crossover point and the "all tested BF thresholds" wording are imprecise. Fix: qualify the claim with the actual sample size at which p<.005 overtakes p<.10 and specify which BF thresholds, rather than "all."

What this audit does not guarantee

  • Stochastic, single-seed runs. The AUCs are re-simulated, not read from shipped data. Values marked ✅ match the manuscript at its reported precision under seed 1; values marked ≈ fall within the seed-drift band (seeds 1–5) and are explicable by simulation noise. The median of AUCp (and of AUCBF, AUCES) is stable to two decimals across seeds.
  • The AUCp == AUCBF equality is exact by construction, not an empirical surprise. For a given t-statistic, log-p and log-BF (two-sample, rscale = 0.707) are strictly monotone functions of t, so the ROCs (and thus the AUCs) are identical. The "equal discriminability" finding is therefore guaranteed by the monotone mapping, not by the data; the paper is correct, and the audit confirms it (RMSE = 0).
  • Bayes-factor prior sensitivity. The code uses the default/medium Cauchy prior (rscale = 0.707). The manuscript's claim that different priors shift the ROC but do not change the AUC was verified: AUCBF is identical for prior odds .1/1/10 (Exp 7) and .25/1/4 (Exp 8), as a prior merely rescales the log-BF (a monotone shift), leaving the AUC unchanged.
  • Figures were not pixel-verified. The numerical claims underlying Figures 2–12 were checked by recomputing the AUCs, distances, and rates; the rendered figure images were not diffed pixel-by-pixel.
  • Second-language recompute not performed this session. All headline quantities were recomputed in R from a fresh invocation of the author's own code. An independent Python/scipy re-derivation (REPRO_STANDARDS §5) was not run and is listed as an optional next step.
  • Cited background numbers (Table 2 verbal categories from Wetzels/Jeffreys; e.g., C29) are definitional/cited and not independently simulable (marked ➖ in the claims inventory).
