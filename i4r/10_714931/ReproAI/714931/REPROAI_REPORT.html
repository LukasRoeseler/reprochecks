Package audited: 10_714931 — paper.pdf + paper_extracted.txt, plus the JOP replication package on Harvard Dataverse (10.7910/DVN/D2WL5I).

Mode: static audit + independent R 4.6.1 reimplementation. The authors' replication files were obtained from the JOP Data Archive: a Stata .tab dataset (1,501 obs × 32 vars) and two Stata 15.1 .do files (main manuscript + online appendix). Stata 15.1 is not installed on this host, so the models were independently re-estimated in R (ordinal::clmm for ordered-logistic random-intercept models and lme4::glmer for the binary models), porting the exact Stata meologit/melogit specifications (random intercept by municipality, factor base levels i.b0./i.b1., continuous controls, and the margins, predict(mu fixedonly) logic). This is a faithful port, not a byte-for-byte Stata reproduction; version/method gaps are flagged below.

Paper type: cross-sectional public-opinion survey, multilevel (individuals nested in municipalities) ordered-logistic policy-feedback study with propensity-score-matched robustness. It is observational, not a difference-in-differences/event-study design (no pre/post panel on the outcome).

Originals: untouched. Reference copy in extracted/, all work on copies in exec_check/.

Headline result reproduces. The replication package is complete and internally consistent, and the paper's central claims — that the female×WPS and female×WPS-age interactions are negative and significant, that men's predicted probability of condemning VAW and of supporting bystander intervention rises substantially in WPS municipalities, and that IPV incidence is lower in WPS municipalities — all reproduce from the shipped data in an independent R reimplementation, both in coefficient sign/significance and in ~1–2 pp of the marginal predicted probabilities. Descriptive sample facts (N=1,501; 100 muni; 46% WPS muni; 49.6% of interviews in WPS muni; 66.5/78.5% and 61.4/67.5% agreement shares; >50% know an IPV victim) check out. No P0 or P1 findings. The one P2 finding is a prose inconsistency: the bystander Results paragraph attributes the significant interactions to "Models 2 and 8" when they are Models 6 and 8 (Model 2 belongs to the other dependent variable). Three P3 nits (a "Table 2" caption typo, a minor sample-size reconciliation on one appendix model, and a 0.1-pp rounding item). Nothing contradicts the paper's conclusions.

Findings (for the authors)

P2 — medium

ADV-001 — Bystander Results paragraph attributes the significant interactions to the wrong model numbers. The text states "Models 2 and 8 reveal a negative and statistically significant interaction effect between gender and the WPS and years since WPS creation variables" when describing the bystander-intervention dependent variable (paper_extracted.txt:588). For the bystander DV the WPS and years interactions are Model 6 and Model 8; Model 2 is the intolerance DV's WPS-interaction model (the same text at line 449 correctly attributes Model 2 to DV1). These are distinct dependent variables (Cronbach α = 0.29; the paper treats them separately), so this is a genuine prose-vs-table inconsistency, though it changes no statistic or conclusion. Fix: change "Models 2 and 8" to "Models 6 and 8".

P3 — low

ADV-002 — Figure 4 caption references a nonexistent "Table 2". "Results based on model 8 in Table 2" (paper_extracted.txt:638); there is no Table 2 — all eight models are in Table 1. Caption typo only. Fix: "Table 1".

ADV-003 — Table A3 sample-size reconciliation: the IPV-experience model is printed with N = 688, but an independent run on the same shipped dataset yields 697 usable observations. The WPS coefficient reproduces well (−0.854 vs −0.865), so the 9-observation gap has no material effect; it is most plausibly a listwise-deletion/quadrature difference between R's glmer and Stata 15.1's melogit. Fix: run the shipped .do end-to-end in Stata; report the exact analysis N and missing-data note (688 vs 697).

ADV-004 — DV1 agreement shares printed one 0.1-pp low (66.5% / 78.5% vs recomputed 66.6 / 78.6). Rounding nit on descriptive text only. Fix: print with consistent rounding.

What this audit does not guarantee

  • No live Stata execution. Stata 15.1 was not available on the host; the models were re-estimated in R 4.6.1 from the shipped .tab with identical specifications. All headline statistics independently reproduce in sign, significance, and ~1–2 pp magnitude, but a genuine Stata re-run of the shipped .do files was not attempted. If desired, the author's code against the shipped dataset on a Stata 15.1 instance would close the residual quadrature/N gaps.
  • Main-text Table 1 (models 1–8) is not present in paper_extracted.txt (the PDF table was not captured by text extraction). Headline interactions were verified through the structurally identical appendix models (A6–A10) and the R reimplementation; the literal Table 1 block could not be diffed cell-by-cell.
  • Background/third-party figures (UNODC 87,000 femicides; Perova & Reynolds ~17% femicide reduction; SENASP 8.2/61 NGO support; IBGE 8.3% / ~460 WPS; 143 VAW-law muni) are correctly cited but not independently recomputable from the shipped package.
  • Cronbach's α = 0.29 between the two ordinal DVs was not recomputed (secondary, item-pair).
